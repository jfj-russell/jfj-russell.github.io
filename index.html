About me

I am a physicist interested in understanding the dynamics of physical systems through optical measurements.

I will shortly begin a PhD at IMDEA Nanociencia in Madrid, where I will use time-resolved terahertz spectroscopy to investigate ultrafast charge-carrier dynamics in semiconductors.

Before this, I completed an MSc in Physics with Quantum Dynamics at Imperial College London. For my master’s research, I worked in the Quantum Measurement Lab with Dr Michael Vanner, using heterodyne spectroscopy and numerical modelling to investigate multimode Brillouin scattering in optical fibre and the temperature dependence of the spacing between acoustic-mode resonances.

I studied physics at King’s College London, including an exchange year at the University of California, Berkeley, and graduated with First Class Honours. My bachelor’s thesis was carried out in Atto@Kings with Dr Emilio Pisanty and Dr Anne Weber, where I studied electron trajectories and quantum-path phases in two-colour high-harmonic generation, with a focus on tunnelling dynamics in strong laser fields.

Earlier, through a King’s Undergraduate Research Fellowship, I worked in the Levitated Nanophysics Group with Professor James Millen. I developed particle-tracking software and used spectral analysis and numerical modelling to investigate the gradual loss of stability of microscopic diamond particles in a modified Paul trap.
